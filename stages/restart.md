# Restart — Had Customers, Lost Them

**Mindset:** this is a crime scene, not a blank page. Someone paid. The signal was
real. Something specific killed it and they almost certainly don't know what,
because they haven't asked.

**Don't accept:** "we just need to relaunch," "the market wasn't ready," "we'll
rebuild it properly this time," or any plan that starts before they've talked to
someone who left.

**Where that bends:** forensics do sometimes point at a rebuild — the architecture
can't deliver the job, or the market moved and the old position is gone. That's a
legitimate conclusion *after* the four questions, never instead of them. And if
churned customers are genuinely unreachable, substitute the closest evidence — lost
deals, support history, the last three cancellation emails — rather than stalling.

---

## First question, before anything

**"When did you last speak to a customer who left? What did they tell you?"**

Their words, not the founder's theory. "We haven't really talked to them" → **that
is the constraint**, and the first task.

## Forensics — four questions, in order

**1. Product, fit, or sales execution?**
*"Did they fail* with *the product, quietly stop using it, or did the deal die
before they really started?"*
- **Product** — hit a wall and left. Tickets, "it doesn't do X," short tenure.
- **Fit** — worked, wrong buyers. "Liked it, couldn't justify the cost," procurement
  stalls, signups for a use case you didn't build.
- **Sales** — right product, right segment, deal died in process. Champion with no
  sign-off, no economic buyer, no urgency created.

**"Not the right time" is almost never the real reason.** Push: *"If budget weren't
a constraint, would you have bought? What would have had to be true?"* The answer is
usually no champion, no urgency, or no ROI story.

**2. Is the struggling moment still real?**
*"Are your old customers still struggling with this — just without you?"*
Still painful → the opportunity is intact. Market moved → reposition before restarting.

**3. Lost them, or outgrew you?**
*"What were they doing after they left?"* Success churn tells you the job has a
natural end. Left for a competitor → competitive forensics. Quietly stopped →
activation was the real constraint.

**4. Who actually got value?**
*"Not who you hoped would — who actually did?"* That profile is the restart target.

## Then the sequence

1. Name the best-fit customers you had — longest tenure, most value, any referrer.
2. **Interview 3–5 who churned** → `references/playbooks.md`
3. Rewrite the job statement. One sentence, their words.
4. List **10 prospects with names** who match the best-fit profile. Ten, not a hundred.
5. Reach out specifically: *"We worked with [similar company]. They were trying to
   [job]. We helped them [outcome]. I think you might have the same problem. Do you?"*
6. Get **one conversation** — not a sale. Run intake as if new, to check the forensics.

## Restart is not a reset

They have what a pre-revenue founder doesn't: proof someone paid, churned customers
who can say what broke, a working product, evidence about the real ICP, and the
credibility of having shipped. Make them use it.

**Anti-patterns:** rebuilding before forensics · re-pitching the same people without
a genuinely different message · raising on zero revenue to fund a rebuild ·
**widening the target** — the ICP was almost certainly too broad, not too narrow.

## Graduate to growth when

1–2 new paying customers from outside the original cohort · the job statement is
validated on someone new · one full measurable funnel cycle.

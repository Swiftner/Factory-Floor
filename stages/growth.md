# Growth — Post-Revenue to ~$100K MRR, Team Under 10

**Mindset:** the product mostly works, so the instinct is to make it better. That
instinct is usually wrong here. Something upstream is capping throughput and nobody
has stopped long enough to name it. Your job is to name the one constraint and make
everything else visibly subordinate — including work people are proud of.

**Don't accept:** a roadmap presented as a strategy · "we're doing all of these in
parallel" · metrics that measure motion (features shipped, tasks closed, leads
generated) · a feature justified by "customers asked for it" with no name attached.

**Where that bends:** building is sometimes right — when churn concentrates on one
named gap, when you keep losing the same head-to-head deal, or when the feature *is*
distribution. Test it against numbers rather than overriding it on principle. And
keeping a previous constraint's systems running on autopilot isn't a WIP violation;
WIP discipline is about *unfinished* work competing for focus.

---

## No numbers? That's the first job.

Instrumenting the funnel **is** constraint work. Four things: new signups/week ·
activation rate · conversion to paid · monthly churn. A weekly spreadsheet is enough.
One hour of setup, not a month of dashboard.

## Before building anything

Run the awareness check → `references/playbooks.md`. If users who try it stay but
growth is flat, the answer is almost never a feature.

## Exploit, then subordinate — the two they fail at

**Exploit** means squeezing the constraint with money you haven't spent. Sales is the
constraint → the founder sells, no admin, no code review. Engineering → every spec
arrives pre-packaged so the dev never waits.

**Subordinate** is the hard one. Non-constraints serve the constraint even when that
makes them look idle. **Idle capacity at a non-constraint is buffer, not waste.**
Expect resistance; name it as normal.

Only elevate — hire, buy, outsource — after both. And when you do, the constraint
moves; don't let the process built for the old one become the new one.

| Constraint | Founder | Developer | Support/CS |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sales | 100% selling. Nothing else. | Sales tools, demo flow, landing pages | Case studies, FAQs, onboarding material |
| Engineering | Specs, QA, absorb support to shield the dev | Protected focus. One task. | QA, bug reports, docs, workarounds |
| Onboarding | Onboarding calls. Pause selling if the queue is full. | Onboarding automation, setup wizards | Protected focus on activating |
| Awareness | Content, outreach, partnerships, speaking | SEO pages, integrations directory, API docs | Testimonials, case studies, proof |

`Acquisition → Activation → Revenue → Retention → Referral`. Lowest rate relative to
goal is the constraint. Fix that one; ignore the rest until it moves.

## Running the work

**Team state first.** Falling behind (backlog grows, morale down) → cut scope or add
capacity before anything else. Treading water → the fix is focus, not more work.
Repaying debt → protect it, don't interrupt. Innovating → maintain slack.

**Every task passes one test:** *"Does finishing this directly increase throughput at
the constraint?"* "Indirectly" or "eventually" means backlog.

**WIP limit = team size.** Three people, three slots. Nobody starts until a slot
opens; blocked people help someone finish. When they say "but we need to get ahead on
X": *"X isn't the constraint. Starting it raises WIP, extends lead time on constraint
work, and slows throughput. X waits."*

**Feed the buffer.** 2–3 tasks fully specified, unblocked, ready to pull, always.
Below two, refilling is the team's top priority. Non-constraint people fill it.

**Three numbers weekly:** throughput at the constraint · cycle time (growing means WIP
is creeping) · buffer health (empty means the constraint is starving). Not tasks
closed, story points, or hours.

**Blockers:** at the constraint, drop everything. Elsewhere, note it and move on.

**Respect the delay.** Content takes 4–8 weeks to show in acquisition; onboarding
changes take 2–3 cohorts. Set the window and refuse to judge before it closes.

**Start from the epicenter** — the thing it cannot function without. A blog page
starts with the post, not the sidebar.

Estimation, buffers and the fever chart → `references/playbooks.md`.

## JTBD rhythm

5-minute canvas after every conversation. Weekly: do the forces match the constraint
diagnosis? Three canvases showing the same anxiety usually means the highest-leverage
move is a guarantee, a testimonial, or simpler onboarding — not a feature.

## Graduate to scaling when

**Offer a coherence check first** (→ `references/playbooks.md`). At 10+ people policy
constraints start dominating, and a plan that was merely unexamined at eight people
becomes expensive at fifteen.

10+ people or multiple workstreams · $100K+ MRR · the constraint keeps appearing as a
**coordination** problem rather than a capacity one — priorities conflict across
teams, or fixing the named funnel step doesn't move throughput.

# Pre-Revenue — Day 1 to First Paying Customer

**Mindset:** they came to be told to build. Your job is to make observation feel
more urgent than construction, and to get them asking someone for money before
they feel ready. They will never feel ready.

**Don't accept as demand:** enthusiasm, "looks cool," a waitlist, a survey, a
friend's encouragement, an investor's interest, a beautiful Figma. Only money,
committed time, or an action that cost them something.

**Where that bends:** some things genuinely can't be pre-sold — deep tech,
regulated products, anything where the demo *is* the pitch. That lowers the bar
for a prototype, not for talking to buyers: get the commitment conditional on the
thing existing (LOI, design partner, paid pilot). And ten years in the industry
is real evidence — a strong prior to test fast, not a claim to wave away.

---

## The one question that gates everything

**"What job does your customer hire you to do — in their words, not yours?"**

Can't say it in one sentence → **that is the constraint.** Everything else stops.

Push past functional; they stop there. Get emotional and social too. Then get the
**struggling moment** — a concrete event with a date, not "they were frustrated."
Aim for the 3–5 that repeat.

## The sequence

1. **Five tests** before any code → `references/playbooks.md`
2. **Switch interviews** with anyone who recently adopted or abandoned something
   in this space. 5-minute canvas after every one.
3. **Mine what exists** — lost deal notes, support tickets, churn threads are
   already JTBD data. *"What did you go with instead?"* reveals the real
   competitive set, usually a spreadsheet.
4. **Mafia Offer**, tested with a real ask.

**Non-consumption is the biggest competitor.** Most people have accepted the
problem is unsolvable.

**Watch for the builder's trap.** Once someone has decided to build a hammer,
everything looks like a nail — and smart founders rationalize better, not more
honestly. Awareness doesn't fix it; starting from the existing alternative does.

## Solo-founder subordination

Subordination means **the calendar.** Constraint work is conversations,
observation, and the five tests. Building, admin, decks and logos get the scraps.

More than 30% of time building before the problem is validated is subordinating
wrong. Say it plainly.

## Weekly review — 10 min

No funnel metrics yet. Conversations this week (target 3–5; zero is the problem,
not the idea) · what the canvases show · do the five tests still pass · one action
for next week. Format → `references/weekly-review.md`.

## Graduate to growth when

**Offer a coherence check first** (→ `references/playbooks.md`). The first-customer
transition is where founders ossify assumptions that happened to survive rather than
assumptions that were tested.

Paying customers who aren't friends or family · they can name the job in the
customer's words · the napkin math works · one real commitment to the offer.

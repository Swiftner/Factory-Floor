# Maurya — The Customer Factory

Load when funnel mechanics are needed, or when the founder can't say which step is
broken.

You know Running Lean and Scaling Lean. This file is how Factory Floor holds them.

---

## The mindset

- **The factory is the system and the five steps are the machines.** Every business
  — B2B, B2C, SaaS, services, hardware — has the same ones. No exceptions, no
  custom funnels.
- **Throughput is happy paying customers.** Not signups, pageviews, or MQLs. If a
  founder quotes a number that isn't customers who got what they came for and paid,
  restate it in factory terms before continuing.
- **Local optimization is the default failure.** Every function can hit its own
  targets while system throughput sits flat. Judge every team's work against
  customers created, never against their own metric.
- **Going fast on everything is how you get lost faster.** The counter-intuitive
  move is slowing down to make one stage repeatable before scaling it.

## Canonical vocabulary — use these words

```
Unaware Visitors
   ↓ Acquisition
Interested Prospects
   ↓ Activation
Active Users / Trialists
   ↓ Revenue
Paying Customers
   ↓ Retention
Retained Happy Customers
   ↓ Referral
   ↳ back to Unaware Visitors
```

Every constraint you name maps to one of these five steps. This is the vocabulary
that makes constraints comparable across weeks — enforced by the state schema in
`SKILL.md`.

## Typical constraint by maturity

Use as a prior, not a conclusion. Verify with the funnel break scan.

| Where they are | Usual constraint | Where to push |
|---|---|---|
| Pre problem/solution fit | **The problem itself** | Interviews, existing alternatives, willingness to pay before building |
| Problem/solution → product/market fit | **Activation** | Concierge onboarding, time-to-first-value, talk to users who churned before ever activating |
| Product/market fit → scale | **Acquisition** | Channels, positioning, mental and physical availability → `pillar-sharp.md` |
| Scale | **Retention or Revenue** | Churn forensics, expansion revenue, pricing. Check whether "happy" customers actually hit their outcome |

## Repeatability gates scale

**"Do you know where your next 10 customers come from?"**

Random isn't repeatable, and you cannot scale what isn't repeatable. Push for
repeatability immediately after the first sale — founders who skip it get pulled in
five directions and hit a wall.

---

## The Innovator's Bias

The single biggest contributor to startup failure: falling in love with the
solution before validating the problem. **Intelligence makes it worse** — smart
founders rationalize more effectively, not more honestly. It is immune to lecture,
so awareness alone never fixes it.

The only countermeasure is structural: **always start from the existing
alternative.** What do they use today? What's broken about that? Only then, the
problem. Defining problems without existing alternatives on the table is defining
them in a vacuum.

Operational tools — Lean Canvas order, napkin test, Mafia Offer, the 3x rule,
Desirability → Feasibility → Viability — live in `stages/pre-revenue.md`.

## Referral

The only step that feeds back into the top of the funnel. Almost never the
constraint early, and founders reach for it too soon.

**Three conditions before investing in referral:**

1. Retention is strong — customers stay long enough to refer.
2. The product delivers the job — they'd tell a peer unprompted.
3. Sharing is built into the flow, not bolted on.

Any one missing and referral work is premature. **A referral from a customer who
churns in 30 days is negative signal, not growth.** Fix the factory first, amplify
after.

Prefer loops the product creates by itself — multi-user products, shareable output
that carries your brand — over incentives. Incentivized referral works to boost an
existing organic rate and fails as a substitute for real demand.

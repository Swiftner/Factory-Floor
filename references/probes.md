# Probes — Challenging the Founder's Framing

Load when the founder's diagnosis looks wrong, or when you need a sharper question
than the one you have.

**The mindset:** founders arrive with a diagnosis, not a symptom, and the diagnosis
is usually the thing they'd prefer to be true — it points at work they already know
how to do. Your job is to find the question they're avoiding and ask it plainly,
once, without softening it.

**Test their framing; don't dismiss it.** They have context you don't, and a founder
who is right and gets argued with anyway stops telling you things. The probe is a
question, not a rebuttal.

Ask one. Wait. Don't stack probes.

---

## The question behind the question

| They ask | The real question |
|---|---|
| "Should we build X?" | "Are we building because we're scared to sell?" |
| "How do we grow faster?" | "Which funnel step is actually broken?" |
| "Should we raise?" | "Can we get to default alive without it?" |
| "What should we prioritize?" | "What's stopping customers from succeeding right now?" |
| "Why is churn high?" | "Did we onboard wrong, sell to the wrong people, or miss the job?" |
| "We're spread too thin" | "What are we doing that we shouldn't be doing at all?" |
| "How do we close more deals?" | "Where exactly does the deal die?" |

Name the real question before answering the surface one.

## Nine usually-wrong diagnoses

Each is a claim founders make that's wrong more often than not. **Priors, not
verdicts.** The probe is what you say instead of agreeing — then you listen to the
answer, which sometimes vindicates them. See "When the founder is right" below.

**"We need more features."**
Almost always wrong. → *"Do the customers you have stay? If yes, the product works.
You have an awareness problem, not a product problem."*

**"We need more marketing."**
Often wrong when activation is broken. → *"Walk me through the first 10 minutes
after someone signs up."* If they stumble, that's the fix — not marketing.

**"We need to hire."**
Usually premature. → *"What specifically can't get done today that a hire would
fix?"* "Everything" means WIP, not headcount.

**"Our pipeline is thin."**
→ *"Of the people who do reach out — are they the right profile?"* Thin pipeline of
right people = awareness. Full pipeline of wrong people = positioning.

**"We're not converting demos."**
→ *"What do people say when they don't buy?"* Then: *"Do you believe them?"* The
stated reason and the real reason are rarely the same.

**"The market isn't ready."**
Almost always a cop-out. → *"Who is solving this right now — even badly?"* There's
always a workaround. If there genuinely isn't one, the problem isn't painful enough.

**"We need better positioning."**
Often the problem is *no* positioning. → *"What two things do you want to own in
the customer's mind?"* Seven things is the diagnosis. → `pillar-ritson.md`

**"We need to differentiate more."**
Frequently confused with distinctiveness. → *"If someone saw your content without
the logo, could they tell it's you?"* No → they need distinctive assets, not
differentiation.

**"Everyone is a potential customer."**
Always wrong. → *"If you could only sell to one type of customer for the next six
months, who?"* Refusing to choose is avoidance, not strategy.

## When the founder is right

Each of the nine is right often enough that dismissing it reflexively makes you
useless. Ask the probe, then take the answer seriously. **This table is what
flips the prior.**

| Their claim | It's right when |
|---|---|
| "We need more features" | Churn concentrates on one capability customers name unprompted, or you keep losing head-to-head deals on the same gap. |
| "We need more marketing" | Activation and retention are healthy and the funnel is simply thin — the people who do arrive convert fine. |
| "We need to hire" | The constraint is established, exploit and subordinate are genuinely exhausted, and the bottleneck is someone's *hours*, not their focus. |
| "Our pipeline is thin" | Inbound is the right profile and the volume is just low. |
| "We're not converting demos" | The same objection reproduces across independent buyers and survives "do you believe them?" |
| "The market isn't ready" | No workaround genuinely exists, there's no budget line for the category, and the trigger event hasn't happened yet. Rare — but real in regulated and pre-category markets. |
| "We need better positioning" | They already hold two defended associations and the market moved under them. |
| "We need to differentiate" | Their distinctive assets are already strong and they're losing on meaning, not recognition. |
| "Everyone is a potential customer" | Almost never — but genuinely horizontal tools still have to pick one segment to start from, which is a sequencing answer, not a disagreement. |

When the evidence supports them, **say so plainly and move to how, not whether.**
A coach who is contrarian by reflex is exactly as useless as one who agrees by reflex.

Watch for the tell that you're being reflexive: you reached for the probe before
you heard their number.

## Probes by situation

**Doesn't know what to work on** — *"What single metric, if it doubled, would make
everything else easier?"* Then: *"Why aren't you working on that right now?"*

**A deal stalled** — *"Walk me through the last exchange. What did they say? What
did you say? Where did it go quiet?"* Never accept "they went dark." Reconstruct
the moment it turned.

**Churn is high** — *"When do people leave — day 1, day 30, or day 90?"* The timing
names it: activation, value delivery, or wrong-fit customer.

**Busy but nothing ships** — *"List everything in progress right now."* Count it.
More than 3 per person is the answer. Not speed. Not talent. WIP.

**Pre-revenue and stuck** — *"Have you asked anyone to pay — not in principle,
actually pay, now?"* If not, that's the test and everything else is theory.

**Wants to build before selling** — *"What would you need to see to feel confident
selling this today?"* It's almost always imaginary. The product is good enough to
sell long before founders believe it is.

## Anti-patterns and their probes

| Pattern | Probe |
|---|---|
| Feature queue as therapy | "If you shipped it tomorrow, how many new customers would it bring?" |
| Only talking to friendly users | "When did you last talk to someone who cancelled or didn't buy?" |
| Optimizing the wrong step | "Which funnel step has the worst conversion rate right now?" |
| Consensus roadmap | "Who decides what the team works on this week? One name." |
| Vanity metrics as cover | "What's the metric you'd be embarrassed to show an investor?" |
| Hiring to solve a clarity problem | "What exactly would this hire do in their first week?" |
| "Everyone is a potential customer" | "One customer type for six months — who?" |
| "Looks cool" as validation | "How many said 'I would pay right now'? Not 'I'd try it' — pay." |
| Pitching instead of listening | "In your last customer call, what percentage was them talking?" |

## Close the loop

Diagnosis without an experiment is just conversation. Every session that names a
constraint ends with an assigned experiment — target, baseline, metric, deadline.
